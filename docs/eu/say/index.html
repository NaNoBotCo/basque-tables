Euskara

Say it out loud

Basque is related to no other living language, so there is nothing to carry over from Spanish or French. That makes the spelling look harder than the sound is. Six letters do most of the work.

  • Vowels never slide. Basque has five and they keep their value wherever they fall.
  • Stress is light. Nobody will misunderstand you for putting it in the wrong place; the second syllable is a safe guess.
  • A final -a is often the word for "the": ogi is bread, ogia is the bread, sagardo is cider, sagardoa is the cider.
  • Euskara is related to no other living language. There is nothing to transfer from Spanish or French, which is why the spelling looks stranger than it sounds.

Phrasebook

Twenty-eight things worth having ready

Enough to come in, be fed, raise a glass and admit you have run out of Basque.

Coming in

Kaixo

KAI-sho

Hello

Any hour, anyone.

Egun on

eh-GOON own

Good morning

Until about one.

Arratsalde on

ah-rrat-SAL-deh own

Good afternoon

From lunch until dark.

Gabon

ga-BONE

Good evening

Also goodnight on the way out.

Ongi etorri

ON-ghee eh-TOH-rri

Welcome

What the house says to you, not the other way round. Worth knowing when you hear it.

Manners

Mesedez

meh-seh-DES

Please

Goes on the end: beste bat, mesedez.

Eskerrik asko

es-KEH-rreek AS-ko

Thank you very much

The everyday thanks.

Mila esker

MEE-la es-KEHR

A thousand thanks

Warmer. For someone who has gone to trouble.

Ez horregatik

es oh-rreh-GA-teek

You're welcome

Literally: not for that.

Barkatu

bar-KA-too

Sorry, or excuse me

Getting past someone, or getting attention.

Bai / Ez

bye / es

Yes / no

Bai sounds exactly like the English bye.

At the table

On egin!

own EH-gheen

Enjoy your meal

Said to anyone already eating as you pass their table. Literally: may it do good.

Oso goxoa

OH-so go-SHO-ah

Very good indeed

About food. The one compliment worth having ready.

Beste bat, mesedez

BES-teh bat meh-seh-DES

Another one, please

Works for the drink and the bread.

Kontua, mesedez

kon-TOO-ah meh-seh-DES

The bill, please

Zenbat da?

SEN-bat da

How much is it?

Jan eta edan

yan EH-ta EH-dan

Eat and drink

What the whole institution is for.

At the bar

Topa!

TOH-pah

Cheers!

Glasses up. The one to have ready when the Picon lands.

Osasuna!

oh-sa-SOO-nah

To your health!

Also the name of the Pamplona football club, which is why people grin.

Gora!

GO-rah

Up with —!

Gora zu! — up with you. A toast, a cheer, a shout.

Zorionak

so-ree-OH-nak

Congratulations, or happy birthday

Aupa!

OW-pah

Hey! Come on!

Greeting a friend across a room, or urging on a team.

Coming clean

Ez dakit euskaraz

es DA-keet eus-KA-ras

I don't speak Basque

Said after the first four phrases have raised expectations.

Euskaraz badakizu?

eus-KA-ras ba-da-KEE-soo

Do you speak Basque?

In an American Basque room the answer is often no, and asking is still welcome.

Berriz, mesedez

BEH-rrees meh-seh-DES

Again, please

Ask twice. Nobody minds.

Worth keeping

Adiskide onekin orduak labur

a-dees-KEE-deh oh-NEH-keen or-DOO-ak la-BOOR

With a good friend the hours are short

Carved into a bench on the Basque Block in Boise, in Euskara and English, and photographed on that page here. A diner at Louis' Basque Corner wrote it down in 2015 as well, translating it “time flies when you are among friends”.

Zazpiak bat

SAS-pee-ak bat

The seven are one

Seven provinces. On club walls from Reno to Vancouver.

Maite zaitut

MAI-teh ZAI-toot

I love you

Across a long table with strangers on both sides of you, which is how these rooms were built.

Basquisms

Words English has not got

Nine of them, and not one is a food word by accident.

A word we have not got

hamaiketako

ah-mai-ke-TA-ko

literally: the eleven-o'clock

A second breakfast with a proper name. Around eleven, work stops: a sandwich, often chorizo, and a glass of wine. Not a snack. An institution.

English gets "elevenses" and a biscuit. Euskara gets a sandwich and a drink.

A word we have not got

kuadrilla

kwa-DREE-ya

literally: the squad

The group of friends you fell in with at fifteen and are still seeing every week at sixty. You do not join one and you do not leave one.

The reason a Basque bar at seven o'clock is full of people who already know what everyone is having.

A word we have not got

poteo

po-TEH-o

literally: the potting

Going bar to bar, one small drink and one bite at each, standing, moving on. The pace is the point — you are not settling in anywhere.

A pub crawl minus the crawling. Nobody gets drunk; everybody gets around.

A word we have not got

txikiteo

chi-ki-TEH-o

literally: the little-one-ing

The same round, done on txikitos — small glasses of wine. Older, and more likely to be your grandfather's word for it.

A word we have not got

txotx

choch

literally: the stick

The call in a cider house when a barrel is opened. Everyone drops what they are doing, walks over with a glass and catches the stream sideways.

One word, and forty people stand up.

A word we have not got

bertsolari

behr-tso-LA-ree

literally: verse-maker

Someone who improvises sung verse on a given topic, to a given metre, in front of thousands, in competition, with no notes.

Rap battle, invented early, still sold out.

A word we have not got

herri kirolak

EH-rri ki-RO-lak

literally: village sports

Stone-lifting, log-chopping, ox-dragging, bale-tossing, scythe-racing. Farm work, timed, with a crowd.

Every one of them is a job someone used to do before lunch.

A word we have not got

lagun

LA-goon

literally: friend, and also companion

It does the work of friend, mate, and the person you are with. A table for two is bi lagunentzat — for two laguns.

Which is why this site's Basque button says bi lagunentzako mahaia and not anything about romance.

A word we have not got

amatxi / amona

a-MA-chee / a-MO-na

literally: grandmother, north / grandmother, south

Same woman, two sides of a border. Amatxi in Iparralde, the French side; amona in Hegoalde, the Spanish side.

Which one a Bakersfield family uses tells you which valley they left.

And things they say

They say

Adiskide onekin, orduak labur

a-dees-KEE-deh oh-NEH-keen, or-DOO-ak la-BOOR

word for word: With a good friend, the hours short

Time goes when the company is right.

Carved on a bench on the Basque Block in Boise, and written down by a diner at Louis' in Reno in 2015.

They say

Nolako egurra, halako ezpala

no-LA-ko eh-GOO-rra, ha-LA-ko es-PA-la

word for word: Such wood, such splinter

The chip off the old block.

They say

Otsoa non aipa, han gerta

oh-TSO-ah non AI-pa, han GEHR-ta

word for word: Where you name the wolf, there it turns up

Speak of the devil.

They say

Txakur zaunkaria, ez da hozkaria

cha-KOOR saun-ka-REE-ah, es da hos-ka-REE-ah

word for word: A barking dog is not a biting one

The loud one is not the dangerous one.

They say

Hobe da bakarrik egon, gaizki lagunduta baino

HO-beh da ba-ka-RREEK EH-gon, GAIS-kee la-goon-DOO-ta BAI-no

word for word: Better to be alone than badly accompanied

Better on your own than in poor company.

Which a house that seats you with strangers is quietly betting against.

They say

Goseak dagoenak, ogia amets

go-SEH-ak da-GO-eh-nak, oh-GHEE-ah ah-METS

word for word: The one who is hungry dreams bread

You want what you are short of.

They say

Zahar hitzak, zuhur hitzak

SA-har HEE-tsak, SOO-hoor HEE-tsak

word for word: Old words, wise words

The sayings are old because they were right.

They say

Izena duen guztia, omen da

ee-SEH-na DOO-en GOOS-tee-ah, OH-men da

word for word: Everything that has a name, they say exists

If there is a word for it, the thing is out there.

Said about the old spirits, and quoted about everything since.

ikurriñathe flagRed ground, white cross over a green saltire. Designed in 1894; flown over American Basque clubs since long before it was legal at home.
lauburufour headsFour comma-shaped heads turning around a centre. Cut into gravestones, chests, cheese rinds and pastry lids.
musthe call that opens a new handThe card game. Four players, two pairs, bidding in four rounds, and signals across the table that everyone can see and nobody is supposed to.
ostatuainn, lodgingThe boarding house. Every old room in this directory was one first.
pilotaballThe handball game, played against a wall. Every Basque town has a fronton; so do Boise and Elko.

txokoCHO-kocorner, nookA members' cooking society — a kitchen a group keeps and cooks in for itself.
