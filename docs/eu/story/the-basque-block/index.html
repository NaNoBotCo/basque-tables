The Basque Block

Interfazea euskaraz dago. Testuak ingelesez daude: ez zegoen ingelesetik euskarara itzultzeko makinarik eskuragarri, eta ez da itzulpen faltsurik egin.

Grove Street between Sixth and Capitol, Boise. A pub, a market, a dining room, a club and a museum — and the museum is the boarding house all of it came out of.

One block, five doorscited

Bar Gernika on the Capitol corner. The Basque Market halfway down, cooking paella on the pavement on Wednesdays and Fridays. Leku Ona at the Sixth Street end, with a hotel over it. The Basque Center, which is the club. And the Basque Museum & Cultural Center, in the Cyrus Jacobs-Uberuaga house.

The house came firstcited

Cyrus Jacobs built it in 1864. It became a Basque boarding house, the Uberuagas ran it from 1917 and owned it from 1928, and it took boarders until 1969. Sixteen years later Adelia Simplot opened a museum in it. The block assembled itself around a building that had been feeding Basque men for half a century.

What this does to a dinner for twoinference

In Winnemucca or Gardnerville the boarding house is the restaurant, so the long table comes with the meal. On the Basque Block the boarding house is a museum, and the restaurants beside it seat you at your own table. Same descent, different arrangement — and two people who want a room to themselves will find one more easily on Grove Street than on Highway 395.

Every five years it fills upcited

Jaialdi runs six days and draws 30,000 to 50,000 people to these streets. The last was 29 July to 3 August 2025; the next is 2030. Anyone planning a quiet dinner in Boise that week should know it.
